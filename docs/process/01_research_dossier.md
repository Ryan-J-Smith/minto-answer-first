## The method (for the technique chapter)

| # | Claim | Conf. | Source |
|---|-------|-------|--------|
| M1 | Ideas at any level summarize the ideas grouped below them | A | Book (Logic in Writing) |
| M2 | Vertical relationship: each level answers the question (why? / how?) raised by the level above | A | Book |
| M3 | Horizontal relationship: ideas in a group are the same kind of idea, in logical order (deductive, or inductive by time / structure / degree) | A | Book |
| M4 | MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) — term coined by Minto | A | McKinsey alumni article title; Wikipedia MECE page |
| M5 | Pronunciation "meece" (rhymes with niece) is how Minto says it, per the McKinsey alumni article. Director (a former McKinsey consultant): most people at the firm say "mee-see"; "meece" is fine for the film. | A | McKinsey alumni article title; director confirmation 2026-09-23 |
| M6 | SCQA / SCQ (situation, complication, question, answer) as the introduction | A | Book; Harrison Metal names "the SCQ method" |
| M7 | Minto developed the method at McKinsey in the late 1960s/early 1970s; training became mandatory for incoming consultants | B | thinkinsights.net; Wikipedia. `[CHECK: firm-internal]` for "mandatory". |
