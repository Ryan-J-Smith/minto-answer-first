## First finding: the film is 5:42, not 4:00

The per-scene estimates in the storyboard headers sum to **342 seconds**:
hook 30 + title 4 + thesis 22 + origin 48 + rules 45 + recursion 35 + deck 28 + applications 40 +
reach 42 + reveal 25 + next 18 + credits 5. Every timestamp below assumes that runtime. All three
viewers would be scrolling away from a 5:40 film regardless of quality. This is the single biggest
problem.

## Viewer B — 45, ex-BCG, runs strategy at a bank, skeptical

**Beat by beat**

- Hook. Recognises the setup instantly. "A woman at McKinsey" without naming her: coy. "Taught to
  every consultant since" — sloppy; every *strategy* consultant at the big firms, not every
  consultant. Stays, mostly to see whether the creator gets Minto right.
- Thesis. "Most durable idea in management consulting" — would argue (experience curve? the 2×2?)
  but accepts it as defensible. Notes the three-chapter promise.
- Origin. Genuinely interested; didn't know the "no college degree" or "eight of six hundred" facts.
  "The problem was the thinking, not the language" — nods; that is the real insight. Stays.
- Rules. Accurate to the book. Then notices: **SCQA has been cut from this chapter**, but the reveal
  (10.2) says "This film opened with a situation, a complication, and a question." The film claims a
  structure it never taught. B will catch this.
- Recursion. Likes it. Minor quibble: Minto's stopping rule is "the point the reader will accept
  without asking why", not strictly "a fact". Fine for a film.
- Deck. Recognisable. "Five slides" is tidy but fair as one practitioner's account. 28 seconds of
  material B already knows.
- Applications. "Minto didn't invent breaking problems into parts" — respects the honesty. Would
  push back gently that hypothesis-driven problem solving is more than "questions become claims",
  but accepts the simplification.
- Reach. **This is where B loses respect.** "70,000 alumni, 18 Fortune 500 CEOs, they take the
  pyramid with them" — the McKinsey CEO pipeline is evidence of McKinsey's reach, not Minto's. The
  causal leap is exactly the kind of consulting-content flex B distrusts. "Then teaches it to their
  clients" — some do, framed as universal.
- Reveal. B applies the film's own test and it fails: **the chapters do not open with their answers.**
  Origin opens with a story ("Barbara Minto was working in Cleveland…"). Technique opens with
  "Here's the technique." Reach opens with a *question* ("How far did it reach?"). The narration then
  claims "three supports, each with an answer of its own." B: "No they didn't."
- Next steps. Fine. Would follow the author only if the author showed insider credibility on screen.

**Scroll-away:** none for quality reasons, but would skip at 4:12 because the film is too long.
**Inaccurate / overclaimed:** "every consultant"; "teaches it to their clients" as universal; the
CEO stat as Minto impact; "each with an answer of its own".
**Cosplay:** reach chapter; "the client sees five slides" is fine coming from a practitioner but
needs the practitioner to be visible.

## Combined verdict

### Scores

| Viewer | Score | One sentence |
|---|---|---|
| A (PM) | **5/10** | Strong hook and a genuinely good technique chapter, buried behind a title card, a biography, and a McKinsey flex that together lose them twice. |
| B (ex-BCG) | **6/10** | Accurate and respectful where it counts, but the self-referential claim collapses under its own test and the reach chapter mistakes McKinsey's reach for Minto's. |
| C (analyst) | **5/10** | The middle 90 seconds would be screenshotted and shared; the first 75 seconds and the last 90 make sure most of them never see it. |

### Five changes, ranked

1. **Cut real runtime to ≤ 3:30.** The storyboard is 5:42. Reach → 20 s (one diffusion visual, two
   numbers maximum, drop the CEO stat). Deck → 15 s or fold into rules. Origin → 30 s. Recursion
   loses the three-size coda or labels it. Title card → 2.5 s, or overlay the title on the last hook
   frame instead of cutting to black.
2. **Keep the hook's promise within 60 seconds.** Either put Technique before Origin, or end the hook
   by *showing* the completed one-page pyramid (not three empty boxes) and keep it on screen as a
   persistent element through the thesis and origin so the viewer always sees "the page" they were
   promised.
3. **Make the chapters actually answer-first, and either teach SCQA or stop claiming it.** Each
   chapter's first line must be its governing statement: e.g. Origin: "It came from a secretary
   with no degree, hired as an experiment." Technique: "One shape, three rules." Reach: "It went
   everywhere consultants went." Then the reveal is true. For SCQA: a 5-second beat that re-shows
   the hook with S / C / Q / A tags, or delete the reference from the reveal.
4. **Rewrite Reach so the evidence is about Minto, not McKinsey.** Four decades in print, nine
   languages, taught at every major firm, still taught by her in person — those are hers. Alumni
   counts and CEO tallies are McKinsey's and read as cosplay to A and as a false inference to B.
5. **Put the author in the film.** One line of on-screen text before the credits (a short first-person line from the author) converts C's and A's "useful" into a
   follow, and gives B the practitioner voice that makes "five slides" credible.

Smaller: label the OUT/IN arrows as "communicating" / "solving"; label the three sizes in 06.7
("a sentence", "a slide", "a year"); label the three squares in 09.3 ("firms", "clients"); soften
"The idea is simple. The skill is not." to something that invites rather than gatekeeps.

- **B:** "A former McKinsey consultant on the real reason your recommendation ended up in the
