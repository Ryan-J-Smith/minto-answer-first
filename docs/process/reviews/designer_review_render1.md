Evidence: `out/review/AnswerFirst-sheet-01..04.png` (2 s cadence), per-scene sheets in `out/clips/`,
and eight stills I rendered into `out/review/d/` (01-hook-540, 03-thesis-300, 04-origin-900,

## 1. Grade: 7.5 / 10

It reads as one film: one paper, one ink, one accent, one serif, and the pyramid shape recurs in every
chapter so the self-reference lands without being announced. What holds it back is composition, not
concept: too many frames are top-loaded with dead paper beneath, several beats hold a static frame for
3–6 s while the voice moves on, and the caption box is a second visual system fighting the first.

## 3. System recommendations (one change, every scene lifts)

- **Captions.tsx** — replace the beige rounded box (`rgba(243,238,228,0.82)`, 10/22 padding, radius 6) with no box: 34px Inter 500, ink at 100% for spoken words, `inkSoft` at 70% for unspoken, a 1px `inkFaint` hairline rule 14px above the text spanning the text width, `marginBottom` 76. The box is the only element in the film with a filled tinted surface and rounded corners; removing it makes the captions part of the page rather than a UI overlay. Keep `whiteSpace: pre-wrap`, cap width at 820px so lines break earlier and never run three lines.
- **theme.ts** — add `layout.contentTop = 170` and `layout.contentBottom = 800` and have builders lay out to those rather than eyeballing; today the eyebrow sits at y 100–125 in every scene (good) but content tops range from 150 (thesis grid) to 220 (recursion), which is what makes the film feel top-loaded in some scenes and centred in others. Add `inkDim: rgba(28,35,51,0.55)` for the dimmed state so nobody dims to 30% again.
- **Paper.tsx** — the vignette (`rgba(28,35,51,0.10)` at the edge) plus grain at 0.07 is right; no change. One optional: expose `tone="deep"` usage for the reveal and credits so the two bookends sit on the deeper paper and the chapters on the lighter — a cheap way to mark "outside the deck" vs "inside the deck".
