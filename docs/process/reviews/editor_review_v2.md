# Editor review of script v2 — and script v3

Reviewer stance: documentary editor + former McKinsey partner, adversarial. Source of truth for
facts: `01_research_dossier.md`. Nothing else was edited.

## A. The ten most damaging problems in v2, ranked

1. **The hook does not stop a thumb.** Muted feed: the first three seconds are grey body text
   assembling on beige. That is the visual definition of "scroll past". With sound: "Somewhere right
   now, a smart person is losing an argument" is a self-help cadence, not a story, and the payoff
   ("the order they said things in") arrives in beat 2, after the viewer has left. It also states
   "In 1966 … worked out why" as fact; the dossier (M7) dates the method to the late 60s/early 70s.
   Fix: a concrete, numbered, slightly funny scenario the viewer has lived (the recommendation on
   slide 31; the room gone by slide 6), with giant numerals as the opening image. See v3.
2. **Slide one reads the narration back as text.** Scene 03 puts the exact sentence being spoken
   inside a box while captions repeat it below. That is the failure the director named. Worse, "learn in
   an afternoon" contradicts Minto's own two-day course (I2). Fix: the slide shows structure and
   two-word labels, never the sentence; "explain it in a minute, master it over a career".
3. **The reveal invokes SCQA that the film never taught.** v2 cut the SCQA beat from scene 05, so
   "situation, complication, question" in scene 10 is jargon, and the "rotate into five cards" is a
   flourish with no idea behind it. Fix: the reveal replays the hook's own images as thumbnails
   labelled Situation / Complication / Question / Answer, so the viewer sees the mapping rather than
   being told a term. Cut the card rotation.
4. **Length.** 650 words plus ~60 s of scripted pauses plus title and credits is a five-minute film
   against a four-minute ceiling. v3 is 520 words; pauses are re-budgeted (0.5 s standard, 0.8 s idea
   end, 1.2 s chapter end). At Emma's measured 171 wpm that lands ≈ 3:45. At speed 0.95 it is ≈ 4:00,
   so run speed 1.0 unless she sounds rushed.
5. **Unsupported or overstated claims.** (a) "Most move into industry within a few years" is not in
   the dossier at all. (b) "Taught to every consultant since" — dossier K6 is Minto's own site,
   conf. B. (c) "Fourteen people at a time" is the top of a 10–14 range (I2). (d) "No college degree"
   rests on one paywalled Telegraph piece via Wikipedia (B4). (e) "Eighteen sitting Fortune 500 CEOs"
   is a dated snapshot (Fortune, Oct 2025) and must carry the date on screen. (f) "Learn in an
   afternoon" (see 2). All fixed or flagged in v3.
6. **Written, not spoken.** "In her words: that continues to be the story of my life" is a fragment
   torn from a longer quote and sounds odd aloud. 04.1, 08.3 and 08.6 are 25–30-word compound
   sentences with no breath. Pauses of 1.0–1.6 s after nearly every beat make the voice plod; a real
   speaker varies. v3 shortens sentences and uses three pause lengths.
7. **The running example arrives with no frame.** A viewer sees "Raise the price of the core plan 8%"
   and has no idea whose plan or why it matters. Fix: a one-line document header on screen ("To: the
   CEO · Re: pricing") at the top of scene 05, never narrated. Also, keep the example's evidence
   numbers plausible and few (three per box).
8. **The five-slide spine is inconsistent.** Eyebrows say SLIDE 1 (03), SLIDE 2 (05), SLIDE 4 (09),
   SLIDE 5 (11). Origin has none; technique spans 05–08 but only 05 is labelled. Fix: slide 1 =
   thesis, 2 = origin, 3 = technique (05–08, one eyebrow at 05 that persists as a small marker),
   4 = reach, 5 = next steps. The reveal draws exactly this.
9. **Decorative beats.** 09.9 ("The idea is simple. The skill is not.") as two lines of type is text
   read back; 04.7 is a quote card; 03 is all text. v3 gives each a picture that carries the idea (the
   14-seat room stays on screen for 09.9; 04.7 becomes the rail extending to "today").
10. **Minto is thin on screen.** One portrait, one fragment. Respect means letting her own words
    stand where they are strongest: "the experiment" (her word), "the thinking, not the language",
    and "The pyramid is a tool to help you find out what you think" (K8) as the last thing on
    screen, in the credits. No adjectives about her; let the 8-of-600 grid and the 1973 decision do
    the work.

Firm-internal lines for the director to verify are marked `[CHECK]` in v3. Facts carry dossier ids.

## B. Script v3

Conventions: each row is one synthesised beat followed by `pause` seconds of silence. Picture is
what is on screen during the beat; nothing on screen repeats the narration except captions.
Chapter spine: slide 1 thesis · 2 origin · 3 technique (05–08) · 4 reach · 5 next steps.

Running example, unchanged from v2 (header on screen: "To: the CEO · Re: pricing").
