# Clean-editor review of script v4 (spoken) — and script v5

Stance: a documentary script editor who has also sat through a great many consulting presentations.
Every line below was read aloud at narration pace before it was kept. Facts trace to
`01_research_dossier.md` (ids in brackets). Runtime estimates use the pipeline's measured pace for
`af_heart` (median ~176 words per minute spoken at speed 0.95), the scripted pauses at the current
`pauseScale` of 0.85, a 2.6 s title, a 5 s credit and ~0.3 s of pad per scene.

Structural change from the director, applied here: **scene 07 (the deck anecdote) is cut.** Scene 08
now follows the recursion scene directly. The film's own five-part spine (answer, three supports,
next steps) and the "SLIDE n OF 5" markers are unchanged, since they are the film's structure, not
the anecdote.

This is 640 words. Full sentences at a documentary pace cost about 100 words over the fragment
script, and I could not get below ~620 without the lines sliding back into fragments; every shorter
draft of the origin and rules scenes did exactly that. So the plain answer is: **at the current
settings this script runs about 4:16, and the 4:00 ceiling is reached only by combining speed 1.0
(the pace the previous render already used), pauseScale 0.75, and one 16-word cut**, the
hypothesis-tree sentence in 08.3 ("Turn those questions … a hypothesis tree."), leaving the issue
tree and the work plan. Without the cut, speed 1.0 and pauseScale 0.75 give 4:02, which is the
ceiling for practical purposes. I would not cut further; the director should choose between the
hypothesis tree and the last two seconds.

## D. [CHECK] list for the director

1. 01.3 / 09.2 — "the big firms still teach it" / "the major firms teach it to their new
   consultants": dossier K6 is Minto's own site (conf. B). Confirm against experience.
2. 04.1 — "without a college degree": single-source (B4). Keep or drop.
3. 09.3 — "most move on to industry within a few years": your up-or-out claim, not in the dossier.
4. 05.6 — MECE is synthesised as "meece". Confirm or give the firm's pronunciation.

## E. Three lines I am least sure about

- 01.3 "It fits on a single page, the big firms still teach it, and outside consulting hardly anyone
  knows her name." Three clauses in one breath. A narrator can carry it; if it trips, split after "page".
- 06.2 "Push again, and it's the same again." Deliberately plain; some will hear it as flat. The
  alternative, "and there it is again", is warmer but a touch cute.
- 09.5 "The idea is simple. The skill takes practice." Two short sentences on purpose as the closing
  cadence of the chapter; it is the one place the script uses that rhythm.
